# User Personas

Five primary personas representing the core user segments for iMaVote. Each persona is grounded in observed behaviors from existing governance ecosystems.

---

## 1. DAO Core Contributor -- "Alex"

**Demographics:** 28, software engineer, based in Berlin. Holds governance tokens in Aave, Arbitrum, and a small gaming DAO. Spends 5-10 hours per week on governance.

**Role:** Active member of 2-3 DAOs. Votes on nearly every proposal. Participates in forum discussions, occasionally drafts proposals, and delegates overflow voting power to trusted peers.

**Goals:**
- Stay informed across all active proposals without missing deadlines
- Build reputation as a thoughtful, consistent voter
- Efficiently delegate tokens in DAOs where deep engagement is not feasible
- Create and champion proposals in primary DAO

**Frustrations:**
- Gas costs eat into participation budget, especially during Ethereum congestion spikes
- Tracking governance forums, Discord channels, and Snapshot pages across three DAOs is exhausting
- No single dashboard shows all active votes, deadlines, and delegation status
- Proposal discussion happens in fragmented venues (forum, Discord, Twitter) with no aggregation

**Key Workflows:**
1. Morning check: review all active proposals across DAOs, sorted by deadline
2. Read proposal, review forum discussion, cast vote
3. Delegate tokens in lower-priority DAOs
4. Draft and submit a new proposal with on-chain execution payload

**Success Metrics:** Votes cast per month, proposals authored, delegation relationships maintained, on-time voting rate (no missed deadlines).

## 2. Passive Token Holder -- "Jordan"

**Demographics:** 35, marketing manager, based in Toronto. Acquired governance tokens through DeFi yield farming and airdrops. Minimal crypto-native experience beyond swapping and staking.

**Role:** Holds governance tokens but votes fewer than once per quarter. Aware that governance exists, mildly interested, but overwhelmed by volume and complexity.

**Goals:**
- Delegate tokens to someone trustworthy so votes are not wasted
- Get notified only when a truly important vote is happening
- Understand proposals in plain language without reading Solidity
- Vote in under 60 seconds when choosing to participate directly

**Frustrations:**
- Receives no notifications; discovers votes only after they close
- Proposals read like technical specifications, not actionable summaries
- Delegation options are opaque -- no way to evaluate delegate quality
- Feels like their small token balance makes participation pointless

**Key Workflows:**
1. Receive push notification about a high-impact proposal
2. Read AI-generated plain-language summary
3. Either vote directly with one tap or confirm delegation to a trusted delegate
4. Forget about governance until the next important vote

**Success Metrics:** Delegation rate (tokens delegated vs. idle), notification engagement rate, time-to-vote when participating directly.

## 3. DAO Admin / Multisig Signer -- "Sam"

**Demographics:** 31, full-stack developer, based in Singapore. Core team member at a mid-size DeFi protocol. One of five multisig signers responsible for executing governance outcomes.

**Role:** Creates proposals on behalf of the protocol team. Manages voter registration for permissioned governance rounds. Monitors participation trends. Executes on-chain transactions after votes pass.

**Goals:**
- Create and publish proposals with clear formatting and execution payloads
- Monitor real-time voting progress and participation rates
- Identify declining engagement before it becomes a quorum crisis
- Manage voter allowlists and role-based permissions for gated votes

**Frustrations:**
- No analytics dashboard showing participation trends, voter retention, or quorum risk
- Batch operations (adding multiple voters, creating proposal series) require manual repetition
- Emergency governance actions (pausing a contract) have no streamlined fast-track path
- Difficult to communicate voting results to the broader community in a digestible format

**Key Workflows:**
1. Draft proposal with description, forum link, and executable on-chain actions
2. Publish proposal and set voting window
3. Monitor participation in real time; rally voters if quorum is at risk
4. After vote passes, coordinate multisig execution
5. Review monthly governance analytics: participation rate, voter growth, delegate activity

**Success Metrics:** Quorum achievement rate, average participation per proposal, time from vote close to on-chain execution, voter base growth month-over-month.

## 4. Enterprise Governance Officer -- "Morgan"

**Demographics:** 47, operations director at a 500-member professional association, based in Chicago. No blockchain background. Evaluating digital voting tools to replace paper ballots and email surveys for board elections and member resolutions.

**Role:** Runs 4-6 formal votes per year: board elections, bylaw amendments, budget approvals. Needs legal defensibility, audit trails, and a polished experience that non-technical members can navigate.

**Goals:**
- Run verifiable elections that members trust without requiring crypto wallets or tokens
- Export results as CSV/PDF for compliance records and legal filings
- Brand the voting interface with the organization's identity
- Ensure ballot privacy for board elections while maintaining auditability

**Frustrations:**
- Existing blockchain voting tools assume crypto literacy and wallet ownership
- Enterprise voting platforms (Lumi, Simply Voting) are expensive and offer no transparency
- Cannot explain "blockchain voting" to the board without triggering skepticism
- Needs voter identity verification tied to membership rolls, not token balances

**Key Workflows:**
1. Upload voter roll from membership database (CSV import)
2. Configure election: candidates, voting method (ranked choice, approval), timeline
3. Send branded email invitations with unique voting links
4. Monitor turnout in real time; send reminders to non-voters
5. Close election, generate auditable results, export compliance report

**Success Metrics:** Voter turnout percentage, support ticket volume during elections, time to produce certified results, cost per election compared to legacy tools.

## 5. Protocol Delegate -- "Casey"

**Demographics:** 26, governance researcher, based in Lisbon. Works as a professional delegate, representing token holders across Uniswap, Optimism, and ENS. Compensated through delegate incentive programs and grants.

**Role:** Receives delegated voting power from dozens of token holders. Expected to vote on every proposal, publish reasoning, and maintain transparent communication with delegators.

**Goals:**
- Maintain a public delegate profile with voting history, statement of principles, and performance stats
- Communicate vote rationale to delegators efficiently
- Track delegation inflows and outflows
- Build reputation that attracts further delegation

**Frustrations:**
- No standard delegate profile system -- must maintain separate pages on Tally, Agora, and custom forums
- No accountability tooling: delegators cannot see if a delegate missed votes or changed positions
- No way to publish a single voting rationale that reaches all delegators across platforms
- Compensation tracking across multiple delegate incentive programs is manual and error-prone

**Key Workflows:**
1. Review new proposals across all delegated protocols
2. Research, form position, draft vote rationale
3. Cast votes with published reasoning attached
4. Review delegation dashboard: new delegators, departing delegators, total voting power
5. Publish monthly transparency report with participation stats

**Success Metrics:** Voting participation rate (target: 100%), delegation growth over time, delegator retention rate, community feedback on published rationales.
